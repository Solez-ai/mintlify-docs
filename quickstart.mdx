This guide gets you from an unboxed BrailleBridge device to your first typed character in four steps. You need the hardware device, a USB cable (Micro-USB to USB-A or USB-C depending on your ESP32 module), a phone, tablet, or PC for Bluetooth pairing, and a second computer running Chrome or Edge for the Teacher Software. The entire setup takes less than five minutes.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Power on the device">
    Connect the BrailleBridge device to any USB power source — a phone charger, a laptop port, or a USB power bank — using the device's USB cable. The ESP32 draws its power and serial connection from this same cable.

    Once powered, watch the LEDs:
    - The **Red LED turns on** and stays on. This tells you the device has booted, the firmware is running, and English mode is active.
    - The **Green LED stays off**. You are in English mode by default.

    The device is now ready to pair and translate Braille chords. No button presses, no configuration menus, and no power switch to find — powering via USB is all it takes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pair via Bluetooth">
    On your phone, tablet, or PC, open the Bluetooth settings and scan for new devices. You will see a device named **BrailleBridge** appear in the list of available keyboards.

    Select **BrailleBridge** and confirm the pairing request. The device pairs as a standard Bluetooth HID keyboard — the same class as any wireless keyboard you have used before.

    <Info>
      BrailleBridge requires no drivers, no proprietary receiver dongle, and no manufacturer app. It works out of the box with any Bluetooth-capable device running Android, iOS, Windows, macOS, or ChromeOS. Your existing screen reader (TalkBack, VoiceOver, Narrator, or ChromeVox) will automatically read each translated character aloud as you type.
    </Info>

    Once paired, open any app with a text field — a notes app, a message compose window, or a document editor — and place the cursor inside it. BrailleBridge will type directly into that field.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect Teacher Software">
    On the teacher's computer, plug the same BrailleBridge USB cable into a USB port. The device is now connected over both Bluetooth (to the student's device) and USB Serial (to the teacher's computer) simultaneously.

    Open the Teacher Software in **Google Chrome** or **Microsoft Edge**:

    <Warning>
      The Teacher Software uses the **Web Serial API**, which is only available in Chromium-based browsers. Firefox and Safari do not support Web Serial and will not be able to connect. Always use Chrome or Edge.
    </Warning>

    Once the Teacher Software is open:
    1. Click **Add Student** and enter the student's name.
    2. Select the correct **COM port** from the dropdown (on Windows it appears as `COMx`; on macOS/Linux as `/dev/tty.usbserial-xxxx` or `/dev/ttyUSB0`).
    3. Leave the baud rate at the default **115200**.
    4. Click **Connect**.

    A green connected indicator appears next to the student's name. The live text feed for that student is now active — every character they type will appear here in real time.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Type your first chord">
    With the cursor placed in a text field on the student's Bluetooth-paired device, press **dot 1** (top-left key) and **dot 4** (top-right key) together. Hold them for no more than 180 ms — then release both.

    Here is what you will observe:
    - **Both LEDs flash** briefly (approximately 100 ms), confirming the chord was valid and the character was sent.
    - The letter **'c'** appears in the focused text field on the student's device. Your screen reader announces "c".
    - The letter **'c'** appears in the student's live feed in the Teacher Software on the teacher's screen.

    Congratulations — you have typed your first BrailleBridge character. The chord you pressed (`0b001001`, dots 1 and 4) is the standard Grade 1 Braille cell for the letter C, and BrailleBridge translated it exactly as written.

    From here, continue typing Braille chords to build words. Press the **Space key** (the wider key to the left) to insert a space. Hold the **Shift key** while pressing a chord to access uppercase letters, digits (Shift + A through J = 1 through 0), punctuation, and math symbols. To switch to Bangla, hold **dots 4+5+6** together for 500 ms — the Red LED will turn off, the Green LED will turn on, and the device will begin translating Bangla Bharati Braille chords.
  </Step>
</Steps>
