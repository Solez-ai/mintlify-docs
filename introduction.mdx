BrailleBridge is a low-cost, bilingual digital Braille writing and classroom examination system. Its mission is straightforward: every blind student should be able to write their own exam answers — independently, privately, and in their own language — while every teacher receives readable digital text in real time, without needing to know a single Braille cell. By placing a six-dot mechanical keyboard in the student's hands and a browser-based console on the teacher's screen, BrailleBridge removes the sighted writer from the equation entirely.

## Who is BrailleBridge for?

BrailleBridge is designed for two groups of people whose needs have historically been in tension in the classroom.

**Blind and visually impaired students** who already know Braille and want to write at their own pace, with full privacy, without dictating every word to another person. BrailleBridge gives you a familiar six-dot chord keyboard — the same dot layout you know — and lets you compose answers silently. Your output goes directly to your own device over Bluetooth, where your screen reader can confirm each character aloud in your ear.

**Teachers and exam invigilators** who are sighted and do not read Braille. You do not need to learn Braille to use BrailleBridge. Every character the student types appears instantly in plain, readable English or Bangla in your browser — no transcription, no delay, no specialist knowledge required. You can monitor the whole class at once, expand any student's view, and export a timestamped record at the end of the session.

## The problem it solves

The most common workaround in classrooms today — dictating answers to a sighted writer — creates a cascade of problems for the student. BrailleBridge is designed to address each one directly.

## System components

BrailleBridge consists of two parts that work together.

**The hardware device** is a compact, 3D-printed Braille keyboard containing six mechanical dot keys (Outemu Red switches), a dedicated Space key, a Shift key, two status LEDs (Red for English, Green for Bangla), and an ESP32 Dev Module. The ESP32 runs the firmware that reads chords, translates them, and broadcasts the result over both Bluetooth HID and USB Serial simultaneously. The enclosure is designed in Fusion 360 and printed on a standard FDM printer using PLA filament.

**The Teacher Software** is a zero-installation, browser-based console that runs in Chrome or Edge. It connects directly to one or more BrailleBridge devices over USB Serial using the Web Serial API. Teachers can add multiple students to a roster, monitor all of them side by side in Exam Mode, expand any individual student's feed to a large readable view, and export a full timestamped transcript at the end of a session.

## Key capabilities

  <Card title="Bilingual: English + Bangla" icon="language">

    Pairs with any phone, tablet, or PC as a standard Bluetooth keyboard. No proprietary receiver, no drivers, no additional software needed.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Web Serial teacher console" icon="globe">
    The Teacher Software uses the Web Serial API to read directly from the device in Chrome or Edge. No installation, no database, no server required.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Under $15 total cost" icon="coins">
    The entire bill of materials — ESP32, switches, keycaps, LEDs, perfboard, PLA filament, and hardware — comes to approximately $12, making classroom-scale deployment realistic.
  </Card>
  <Card title="3D-printed enclosure" icon="cube">
    The two-shell enclosure (top and bottom) is modelled in Fusion 360 and printed on any FDM printer. MX-compatible cutouts let the eight Outemu switches snap in without glue or additional hardware.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Classroom exam mode" icon="graduation-cap">
    The Teacher Software's Exam Mode places every connected student in a monitoring grid on a single screen, with per-character timestamps and one-click export to `.txt` for each student.
