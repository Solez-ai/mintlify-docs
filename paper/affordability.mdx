Affordability and repairability are not secondary features of BrailleBridge — they are core design goals that shaped every component choice from the beginning. A device that costs $1,000 is not accessible technology for schools in low-resource settings; it is inaccessible technology with an accessibility label. Every part in BrailleBridge was selected because it is cheap, standard, widely available, and replaceable without specialist tools or supplier relationships. The total bill of materials for one complete device, including the enclosure, comes to approximately **$12** — making school-wide deployment realistic on the kinds of budgets that actually exist in the classrooms where this device is most needed.

## Bill of materials

The table below lists every component required to build one complete BrailleBridge device. Prices are approximate and based on widely available commodity electronics markets; actual costs may vary slightly by region and supplier.

| Component | Qty | Cost (USD) |
|---|---|---|
| ESP32 Dev Module | 1 | $5.00 |
| Outemu Red switch | 8 | $3.00 |
| Keycaps (3D-printed) | 8 | $1.00 |
| LED (3mm) + resistor | 2+2 | $0.30 |
| Perfboard | 1 | $0.80 |
| PLA filament (enclosure) | 30g | $1.20 |
| M3 screws, wire, solder | — | $0.70 |
| **Total** | | **~$12.00** |

The eight Outemu Red switches cover the six Braille dot keys, Space, and Shift. The two LEDs (one red, one green) provide language-mode feedback. The perfboard provides a stable base for wiring the common ground bus. The 30 g of PLA filament prints the two-piece enclosure shell. Including tools and minor overhead, a complete device can be built for under $15.

## Compared to alternatives

The cost difference between BrailleBridge and existing solutions is not a matter of degree — it is a matter of kind. A **Perkins Brailler**, the most widely used mechanical Braille writer, costs **$600–$1,000+** per unit. It is durable, reliable, and accepted in examinations, but it produces only embossed output that most sighted teachers cannot read, and when a component fails, specialist repair is typically required.

A **commercial digital Braille display or notetaker** — the closest comparable digital product — costs **$1,000–$5,000+** per unit. These devices offer sophisticated features, but at that price, equipping even five students exceeds the annual technology budget of many schools in the regions where BrailleBridge is most relevant.

**BrailleBridge costs ~$12.** At that price point, a school can afford to equip every blind student who needs it, keep spare units on hand, and replace failed components the same week they break. This is not just a cost advantage — it is the difference between accessible technology that gets deployed and accessible technology that sits in a catalogue.
